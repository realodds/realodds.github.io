Do crypto day-trading strategies work after fees?

Published 29 October 2026 · Based on our own backtests

Not in our test. All 8 popular day-trading strategies lost money on both SOL and DOGE over two years after fees. Ten of the 16 tests were profitable before costs, and fees turned every one into a loss.

Results: R300 per trade, no leverage, all trades closed by end of day

Costs decide the result

The more a strategy trades, the more it pays. The RSI(2) scalp on SOL made +R365 before costs and paid R1,305 in fees and slippage, ending at -R941. It won 51% of its trades and still lost, because its average loss (R1.49) was bigger than its average win (R0.86).

With 5x leverage every loss above grows about five times; at 10x, VWAP mean reversion on SOL lost R24,436 over the two years on R300 trades.

Could cheaper fees save them?

We re-ran the tests at 0.02% "maker" fees with no slippage. Only the VWAP trend pullback stayed positive in both years on both coins (+R36 on SOL, +R260 on DOGE over two years), and almost all of that came in the first year. Stop-losses usually fill at the higher market-order fee, so even that is optimistic.

How we tested: real Binance price history from 29 Oct 2024 to 29 Oct 2026, R300 per trade, 0.05% fee + 0.02% slippage per side on the full (leveraged) position, 0.01% funding per 8 hours held, and liquidation when a leveraged position is wiped out. Signals act on a closed candle and enter at the next candle's open. Past results do not predict future results.
